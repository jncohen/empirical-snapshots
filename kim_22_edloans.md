Between 2010 and 2022, average student loan balances appear to have changed across under-35 White, Black, and Hispanic households. In 2022, young White households with student debt carried an average of $47,471, compared to $26,927 for similar Black and $21,460 for similar Hispanic households. In 2010, those averages were $36,563, $35,045, and $25,872, respectively, in 2022 dollars. White balances appear to have grown in real dollar terms, while those of their Black and Hispanic counterparts appear to have fallen.

# Context
Student loan debt is concern for both household finances and higher education. Rising tuition costs and limited family economic resources have led many young adults to rely on borrowing to fund their education. There are reasons to believe that student debt promotes economic mobility by increasing educational attainment, but also hinders it by reducing wealth accumulation.  
  
Similar discussions have also focused on racial disparities. Previous research has examined differences in borrowing and repayment rates across racial groups, raising questions about whether the costs and benefits of higher education are distributed equally. This analysis concerns how student debt differs across racial groups, and how these patterns changed between 2010 and 2022 among under-35 households. We ask whether student debt patterns are experienced similarly across groups or whether they reflect disparities that may contribute to broader economic inequality.



# Findings



The figure to the right depicts average student loan balances by race. The survey includes relatively few young Black and Hispanic borrowers, so changes over time could not be clearly distinguished from sampling variation and should be read as suggestive. With that in mind, two observations stand out:
 
**Uneven Student Loan Distribution Across Racial Groups.** White borrowers appear to have experienced the largest increase in average student debt, rising by about 30% between 2010 and 2022. Hispanic borrowers saw a smaller decline of about 17% over the period. Meanwhile, Black borrowers' average balances fell by about 23%, even as the share of young Black households carrying student debt rose from 40% to 53%.
 
**A Racial Divergence in Average Loan Balances.** In 2010, Black and White borrowers held similar average student loan debt, at $35,045 and $36,563, respectively. By 2022, this pattern appears to have shifted: White borrowers held the highest average balances at $47,471, roughly 1.8 times the Black average of $26,927, whereas Hispanic borrowers held the lowest at $21,460.

# Implications 

Student debt appears to have shifted differently across racial groups between 2010 and 2022. These differences raise questions about educational pathways, repayment experiences, and access to financial resources among young adults. Further research could examine borrowing patterns across different types of institutions, such as public, private, and community colleges, as well as different levels of education. Differences in tuition costs and educational opportunities may help explain how much debt students accumulate and their financial outcomes after graduation.
